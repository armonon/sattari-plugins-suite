The idea

One copy of every processor

Most suites ship the same compressor three times over. Ours exists once.

Every processor is a module — the DSP with the plugin-ness removed. A module loads two ways: as its own plugin, or as a slot in a rack. Both run the identical code, so an improvement lands in both at once and they cannot drift apart.

It also means your sessions survive. A module keeps the same parameter names whether it runs standalone or inside a rack, so projects saved before the racks existed still open exactly as you left them.

Sattari Mix

8 slots · in series · AU + VST3

The channel strip you assemble yourself — dynamics, tone and metering, in whatever order the source actually needs.

Each of the eight slots holds one module and has its own bypass. Slot 1 feeds slot 2, and so on down the chain — so you can gate before you compress, or compress before you gate, without owning two different plugins.

An external sidechain runs through the whole rack. Route a kick into it and the compressor ducks a bass to it; the gate opens a pad from it. There's no routing toggle to forget — a routed key is the intent.

Sattari Create

8 slots · in series · AU + VST3

Time, space and damage — reverb, delay, shimmer, tape, risers and vocoding in one rack.

The same eight-slot chain as Mix, different cabinet. Reverbs and delays are tempo-locked: they follow the host, and when the host reports no tempo they fall back to the suite's own tempo bus rather than guessing 120.

Heat and Pulse appear here and in Mix. That isn't a duplicate — it's the same module in two rosters. A module is DSP, not a product.

Sattari Stack

4 layers · in parallel · AU instrument

The instrument rack — and the one that works differently on purpose.

Stack layers rather than chains. Chaining instruments is meaningless: each would write over the last, so only the final slot would be audible. Instead all four layers receive the same MIDI, render at the same time, and sum.

That only works musically if each layer has its own placement, so every one carries level, pan, key range and transpose. The key range is what turns a stack into a split — drums below C3, sampler above — or an octave layer, rather than four instruments in unison.

Latency is the maximum of the active layers, not the sum, because they run side by side. Any layer reporting less than the worst is delayed to match — otherwise a fast instrument arrives early against a slow one and the stack flams on every transient.

Every plugin knows the key

Put Sattari Key on the master. It listens to the whole mix, works out the key, and publishes it to every other Sattari plugin in the session.

Auto Pitch, Harmony, Maqam, VoxKey, Arp, Scaler, Shimmer and Sub Conjurer all follow it — no MIDI routing, no sends, nothing to type in twice. Change the key once and the session moves with you.

Details

Will my old sessions still open?

Yes. Every module keeps the parameter names it had as a standalone plugin, so a project saved before the racks existed loads exactly as you left it. The standalone plugins are all still there too — the racks are an addition, not a replacement.

What happened to Bus, Royal Chain and Vocal Master?

They were fixed orders of processors the suite already had. Rather than keep three plugins whose only content was a chain someone else chose, they ship as presets — "Bus (classic)", "Royal Chain (classic)" and "Vocal Master (classic)". Same starting point, and now you can reorder it.

Why is Stack a separate plugin from the other racks?

Because an instrument and an effect are different plugin types to the host, and because instruments have to be layered rather than chained. Both are decisions that can't be reversed later without breaking saved sessions, so Stack was built as its own host from the start.

Do I need Sattari Key for the rest to work?

No. Every key-aware plugin has a manual key control and works fine on its own. Sattari Key just means you set it once, in one place, and everything follows.

What formats and machines?

macOS, as Audio Unit and VST3, built Universal for both Apple silicon and Intel. Most plugins also ship a Standalone app, and every plugin passes Apple's auval validation.
