# Review packet: the 44.1 kHz Studio-mode vibrato defect and the latency misreport (Track B, proposed for Track A)

Frozen candidate: cbd1ce4. Fix: branch `rnd/next-gen-20260927`. The DSP change is limited to
`plugins/auto-pitch/src/PluginProcessor.{h,cpp}`; no-harm checks 9 and 10 are new release gates.

There are two defects, found in that order. Both are in cbd1ce4, and both are fixed by the same change
to the processor's output stage.

## Defect 1: 44.1 kHz widens vibrato

In Studio mode at 44.1 kHz, a ±40 c, 5.5 Hz vibrato is widened instead of reduced. Measured on the
installed candidate in a real VST3 host (pedalboard), and reproduced offline:
- Classic: -75 / +51 c.
- Natural: -38 / +23 c.

At 32, 48, 88.2 and 96 kHz the same input comes out at about ±17 to 32 c. Steady notes are correct
at every rate.

On real singers at native 44.1 kHz the damage is milder and shows in the tails:
- vibrato depth ratio p90: 1.64 against 1.44 at 48 kHz
- legato settle p90: 400 against 294 ms
- onset shift p90: +2.0 against +0.7 ms

### Root cause

- **The correction the plugin requests is correct at 44.1 kHz.** Per block, the requested
  correction is -0.91 x vibrato with a 12 ms lag at 44.1 kHz, against -0.93 x with 11 ms at
  48 kHz; residuals 2.5 / 2.1 c.
- **Rubber Band R3 renders that moving pitch curve wrongly at 44.1 kHz.**
  - This happens in realtime mode with `OptionWindowShort`.
  - The output contains interfering components: the envelope dips to 4% of its mean (48 kHz: 69%),
    and a spurious sideband appears at -14 dB (48 kHz: -32 dB).
- **It reproduces in a standalone Rubber Band program**, with the vendored 3.3 and with 4.0.
  - Sweeping the correction timing at 44.1 kHz gives errors up to 144 c. That is beyond the ~80 c a
    mistimed correction can physically produce, and every other common rate stays under ~80 c.
  - The standard window avoids it. But it costs 4616 samples of latency and breaks the plugin's
    correction timing at every rate, so it is rejected as a fix.
- **Why earlier benchmarks missed it:**
  - Every accuracy benchmark rendered at 48 kHz.
  - The "offline renderer does not reproduce it" observation was my own test-signal choice, not a
    harness difference. The renderer reproduces it once the level and analysis window match.

## Defect 2: reported latency is wrong at most buffer sizes and rates

This was found by the new host-equivalent check 10, which measures the real delay (noise through the
engine, cross-correlated) at every rate and buffer size.

cbd1ce4 primed its output FIFO when a cushion level was reached. The real delay therefore depended on
the host buffer size and on the content. It matched the reported value only at 32/48 kHz with
512+ buffers.

Actual minus reported delay, in ms (gate: within 2 ms, spread ≤ 0.25 ms):

| rate | reported (cbd1ce4) | 64 | 128 | 256 | 512 | 1024 | reported (fixed) | fixed, every buffer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32 kHz | 1443 | +3.91 | +1.91 | +1.91 | -6.09 | -6.09 | 1635 | -0.78 |
| 44.1 kHz | 2885 | +9.32 | +7.87 | +4.97 | -0.84 | -0.84 | 3038 | -0.77 |
| 48 kHz | 2885 | +8.56 | +7.23 | +4.56 | -0.77 | -0.77 | 3173 | -0.77 |
| 88.2 kHz | 5770 | +15.49 | +14.76 | +13.31 | +10.41 | +10.41 | 6299 | -0.77 |
| 96 kHz | 5770 | +14.23 | +13.56 | +12.23 | +9.56 | +9.56 | 6346 | -0.77 |

- On cbd1ce4, a DAW at 48 kHz with a 64–256 buffer places the corrected vocal 4.6–8.6 ms late.
- At 88.2 and 96 kHz it is 10–15 ms late at every buffer.
- The strict golden glide was 503 samples late. The old golden fingerprint had absorbed that.

## Fix

1. **Qualified-rate engine.**
   - The Rubber Band engine now runs only at rates qualified by check 9: 32 / 48 / 88.2 / 96 kHz.
   - Any other host rate is resampled into the nearest qualified rate (48 kHz, or 96 kHz above
     64 kHz) and back.
   - It uses Rubber Band's own anti-aliased `Resampler` at Best quality, so there is no new
     dependency. The resampler's withheld frames are measured with an impulse at prepare time.
2. **Counted priming at every rate.** This replaces cushion-level priming.
   - The output read head is placed at exactly `input position - target delay`, because the engine
     output stream is index-aligned with its input.
   - The delay no longer depends on buffer size or content, by construction.
   - A fail-safe primes late rather than staying silent if the chain ever lags beyond the target.
     It never triggered in qualification.
3. **Realtime-safe resampler reset** (found by the independent review, below).
   - Rubber Band's `Resampler::reset()` throws its filter away, and the next call rebuilds it with
     heap allocation. That happened on the audio thread at every transport reset and every Live,
     Poly or Transpose toggle.
   - `resetRbFifo` now clears the resamplers by pushing zeros through them. The filter is built
     once, in `prepareToPlay`.
   - A flushed resampler emits its in-flight zeros instead of withholding frames, so the stream
     offset in resampled mode is now the resampler delay. Without that, 44.1 kHz was +2.77 ms late
     (caught by check 10).
4. **6 ms production headroom** is inside the reported latency.
   - The engine's production lag (samples consumed minus samples produced) varies with content. It
     peaks at 3011 samples at 48 kHz in the automation stress.
   - Counted priming with no headroom produced 257 underrun clicks in check 4.
   - With 6 ms the stress results are:
     - 48 kHz: 0 underruns, lag max 3011, minimum FIFO 253.
     - Genuine 44.1 kHz: 0 underruns, lag max 2877, minimum FIFO 241.
     - Genuine 96 kHz: 0 underruns, lag max 6000, minimum FIFO 400.
     - Low voices at 48 kHz (-12 / -19 st): lag max 2994 / 2825, 0 underruns.
   - Caveat: an artificial half-speed, octave-down stress peaked at 3381 and underran with 6 ms. It
     is not a realistic vocal, but it bounds the margin.

## Evidence (Track B build against the frozen candidate)

- **48 kHz real singers:** 96/96 renders (48 CSD tracks, AutoKey off and on, Classic, 512).
  - The final output equals cbd1ce4 delayed by exactly 288 samples (6 ms), bit for bit, from the
    first full output block on.
  - Before that, in the first 224 samples at transport start, the engine's start-up ramp is silent
    instead. That is at most 16 LSB, i.e. below -66 dBFS.
  - So no correction behaviour changes at 48 kHz; only the timing changes.
- **Strict golden:** re-blessed. The new render is the cbd1ce4 render shifted by exactly 215 samples,
  with max diff 0.0 after the shift. AutoPitchCoreContract passes.
- **Check 9, sample-rate vibrato integrity:**
  - 168 renders at 32 / 44.1 / 48 / 88.2 / 96 kHz: Classic and Natural, 4 levels, A2 / A3 / C5, 256 and
    512 buffers, plus semitone settling.
  - It FAILS on cbd1ce4 at 44.1 kHz and PASSES with the fix. At 44.1 kHz:

| | cbd1ce4 | fixed | 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic widest | 64.1 c, asymmetry 14.6 | 19.8 c, 1.0 | 19.8 c, 1.0 |
| Natural widest | 41.9 c, asymmetry 20.0 | 20.9 c, 0.9 | 20.3 c, 0.6 |
| Semitone settle | 30 ms | 15 ms | 25 ms |

- **Check 10, latency truth:** FAILS on cbd1ce4 at every rate and PASSES with the fix (table above).
- **Real host** (pedalboard, VST3), ±40 c vibrato at 44.1 kHz:
  - Classic: -75 / +51 c becomes -21 / +20 c.
  - Natural: -38 / +23 c becomes -22 / +20 c.
- **Real singers at native 44.1 kHz** (vocadito dev), cbd1ce4 then fixed:
  - Vibrato depth p90: 1.64 then 1.46.
  - Settle p90: 400 then 341 ms.
  - Onset p90: +2.0 then +0.1 ms.
  - Within 10 c: 61.8 then 61.0%, which is noise at n = 713.
- **Check 10** also measures the delay after a mid-stream host `reset()`: same value at every rate.
- **Check 4** (automation stress) now also gates Studio FIFO underruns and fail-safe primes. There
  are 0 of both in all 18 runs, at 48 kHz and at genuine 44.1 kHz.
- **New check 11, FIFO headroom at transpose extremes:**
  - Transpose to Key at -12 / -7 / +7 / +12 st, primed while already transposed, plus live changes
    0 / -12 / +12 / -12 during playback.
  - 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz at buffers 64, 256 and 512.
  - Result: 0 underruns and 0 fail-safe primes. This settles the reviewer's worry that the 6 ms
    headroom is too small at pitch scale 0.5.
- **New check 12, audio-thread allocations:**
  - Counts heap allocations inside `processBlock` and `reset()` across a host reset and Transpose
    on/off.
  - Resampled rates (44.1, 22.05, 176.4 kHz) and native 48 kHz: 0 each.
  - Mutation test: with the old `reset()` calls put back it FAILS, with 36 allocations per rate.
- **No-harm checks 1-7:** all PASS (state, presets, AutoKey, automation stress, rate x block, bypass,
  mono/stereo).

## Independent review (2026-09-29) and disposition

A separate reviewer read the diff against the Rubber Band source.
1. **Bug: resampler filter rebuilt with allocation on the audio thread.** Fixed (Fix 3), and gated by
   check 12.
2. **Risk: the fail-safe primed with zero margin.** Fixed: it now keeps the headroom in the FIFO. It
   is also counted (`getStudioFifoStats`) and gated to 0 in checks 4 and 11.
3. **Risk: 6 ms headroom at Transpose -12 st.** Tested by check 11: no underrun, no fail-safe. But
   the test exposed the open defect below.
4. **Nit: per-channel output counts assumed equal.** Unchanged; the same assumption predates this
   change.
5. **Nit: latency fields stale across re-prepare.** Fixed: they are zeroed at prepare, and the
   headroom is set in both branches.

## Open defect found on the way (predates the fix): Transpose-to-Key timing

- With Transpose to Key on, the reported latency is the correction-mode figure, but Rubber Band's
  real delay moves with the transposition. At 48 kHz / 512:

| transpose | cbd1ce4 | fixed |
|---|---|---|
| 0 st | -0.77 ms | -0.77 ms |
| -1 st | +8.65 ms | -1.33 ms |
| -3 st | +10.46 ms | +1.94 ms |
| -6 st | +17.15 ms | +8.25 ms |
| -12 st | +26.69 ms | +19.42 ms |
| +3 st | +7.08 ms | -5.08 ms |
| +12 st | -5.40 ms | -17.40 ms |

- So a transposed beat or instrumental lands off the grid by up to about 20 ms in either build.
  Correction mode is not affected: its ratios stay near 1.
- Fixing it needs a transposition-dependent stream offset, and more reported latency while
  Transpose is on (about +20 ms to cover -12 st). That is a latency decision for the owner.
- Check 11 reports this timing but does not gate it, until that decision is made.

## Trade-offs, reported before merge (owner decision needed)

- **Reported latency rises by the 6 ms headroom** at every rate. At 44.1 kHz the resampled path adds
  the resampler as well:

| rate | cbd1ce4 | fixed |
|---|---|---|
| 32 kHz | 1443 (45.1 ms) | 1635 (51.1 ms) |
| 44.1 kHz | 2885 (65.4 ms) | 3038 (68.9 ms) |
| 48 kHz | 2885 (60.1 ms) | 3173 (66.1 ms) |
| 88.2 kHz | 5770 (65.4 ms) | 6299 (71.4 ms) |
| 96 kHz | 5770 (60.1 ms) | 6346 (66.1 ms) |

  This breaks the standing "not above 2885 at 48 kHz" rule. The alternatives are:
  - **(a) +6 ms (recommended).** It covers every realistic stress with a margin of at least 241
    samples.
  - **(b) about +14 ms.** This also covers the synthetic half-speed case.
  - **(c) keep 2885.** That means cushion priming, and cbd1ce4's 4-15 ms misreport stays.
- **CPU:** unchanged at qualified rates (same engine path). At 44.1 kHz it is +4% (offline render,
  7.07 to 7.35 s per 60 s of audio), which is still below native 48 kHz.
- **176.4 / 192 kHz** now resample to 96 kHz. Timing at 176.4 kHz is noisy, so these rates are NOT
  to be claimed until qualified separately.
